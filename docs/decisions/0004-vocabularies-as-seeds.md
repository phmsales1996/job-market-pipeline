# 0004. Vocabularies are translated through seed tables

Status: accepted, October 2026

## Context
Each source has its own words for workplace type, pay period, employment type and country.
They have to become one vocabulary, and the mapping has to be reviewable.

## Decision
A mapping is data, not code: a seed (a CSV in the repository) keyed on the source and the
source's word, giving the canonical word. Every translated column follows the same pattern:
1. the source's own word is kept beside the translation, in a `*_raw` column;
2. `accepted_values` on the canonical column, with the same list in every source;
3. a test that returns rows where the source said something and the translation is empty.

A word that equals its translation still goes through the seed (`month` to `month`, `BR` to
`BR`). The join is then validating rather than translating.

## Why
- The third test exists because the second one cannot see an empty value. Two real cases:
  a typo in a seed's source column left about a thousand hourly postings without a pay period,
  and a join copied from another model looked up the wrong source's rows and translated
  nothing for 23,000 postings. Both builds were green until that test existed.
- New values arrive constantly. In four days these tests caught a first posting in a country
  not yet seen, a territory the ISO list spells differently, a new pay period (per day), and
  a company that wrote two regions in the country field.
- An identity mapping that skips the seed would let the next unseen word straight into the
  canonical column.

## Consequences
- A new value fails the build until someone decides what it means. That is the intent, and
  it is why the models should be built on a schedule rather than by hand.
- The country seed is generated by a script from the values seen in the data, matched against
  the ISO list. It therefore knows only countries already seen; keeping the whole ISO list is
  a planned change.
